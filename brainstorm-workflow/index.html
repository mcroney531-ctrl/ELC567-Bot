Workflow Brainstorm

We all have that ONE THING.

The task you do every week that eats an hour and teaches you nothing new. The one you'd hand off tomorrow if you had someone to hand it to.

You do have someone. You just haven't briefed them properly.

What you'll walk out with: a master prompt — a specific, reusable brief you can paste into Claude, ChatGPT, or Copilot and put to work on that task immediately. Five steps, about fifteen minutes.
See the whole thing done first

Maya manages client success for a mid-size agency. Here's her run through all five steps.

Step 1 — The problem

"Every Monday I spend two hours building status updates for eleven clients. I'm pulling the same numbers from the same places and rewriting the same sentences with different names in them."

Step 2 — The workflow

  1. Pull last week's delivery numbers — Asana, Harvest
  2. Check the shared inbox for anything unresolved — Gmail
  3. Write a 4-paragraph update per client — Google Docs
  4. Reformat into the client's preferred channel — Gmail, Slack

Step 3 — Her first draft prompt

Auto-built from what she typed above. Honest, but generic — it describes her problem without telling the AI what "good" looks like.

Step 4 — The refinement conversation

The coach pushes on the vague parts: Which step would you hand off first? What does a great update actually look like? What are you not willing to automate? Maya realizes the drafting is the drain, but the "here's what I'd watch next week" line is the part clients actually read — and that line is hers to write.

Step 5 — Her master prompt

## CONTEXT
I write weekly status updates for 11 agency clients. The numbers
come from Asana and Harvest; the writing is the same four
paragraphs with different names in them.

## WHAT I NEED YOU TO DO
Take the raw numbers I paste and draft paragraphs 1-3: what
shipped, what's in flight, what slipped and why. Match the tone
of the sample updates below.

## WHAT STAYS WITH ME
Paragraph 4 — the "what I'd watch next week" call. Never write
that one. Leave it as [MAYA'S CALL] and I'll fill it in.

## OUTPUT I EXPECT
Plain prose, 4 short paragraphs, no bullets, no headers, under
200 words. Client-facing tone: direct, no hedging, no filler.

## THINGS YOU NEED TO KNOW
Never invent a number. If a figure is missing from what I paste,
write [MISSING: metric name] and keep going.

Two hours became twenty minutes. The judgment stayed with Maya.
